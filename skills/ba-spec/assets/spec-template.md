> **Do not edit this file inside a project.** It is installed from
> [ba-tools](https://github.com/Relevant-S/ba-tools) (`skills/ba-spec/assets/spec-template.md`).
> Project facts — products, roles, id numbering, Jira, code branches — live in the project profile,
> `docs/BA/specs/.ba-profile.yml`. Change the template in ba-tools and reinstall.

# Spec template

## Why it is shaped like this

The chain on NWE runs **spec → PRD → epics → stories → build**, and the developer works from the
story. So this template is designed from the far end: every section exists because something
downstream needs it, or because a tester will hit it and nobody wrote it down.

Three things drove the design.

**The story already tells us what it needs.** Our stories carry Acceptance Criteria, an explicit
"do NOT build" list, Tasks, and Dev Notes naming existing files to mirror or reuse at a given
commit. `epics.md` adds a FR Coverage Map, a NonFunctional Requirements section, and a "deliberate
removals" list so dropped things are not read as regressions. A spec that does not feed those makes
someone re-derive them.

**Edge cases are the biggest gap in all three approaches.** Measured across 59 AcmeLending specs and
31 NWE specs, none has an edge-case section, and coverage is incidental: permission-denied paths
appear in 3% and 6%. Section 4 is the fix, and it is a checklist rather than a method.

**Two sections nobody currently writes.** Non-functional requirements, and what the user who is
*not* allowed to do the thing sees.

## The template

Copy everything below the line, then **delete every section your `size` does not need**. Section 16
is the gate at every size.

| Size | Typical change | Sections | Rough length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **xs** | a label, a field, a bug fix | 1 · 3 · 5 · 6 · 16 | under 60 lines |
| **s** | a small flow change, a price or text change | 1–9 · 14 · 16 | under 150 lines |
| **m** | a screen, a rule set | 1–9 · 13 · 14 · 16, plus 10–12 when their trigger applies | under 250 lines |
| **l** | a module, a new money flow, a new surface | all 16 | under 400 lines |
| **xl** | several modules | split it into several `l` specs | — |

*If a spec runs past its rough length, cut before adding. The trial specs grew by 15–65% at m and l,
mostly in sections 4, 7 and 14. Those sections earn their space when they find something; when a row
has nothing to say, "n/a" is the whole entry.*

```yaml
size: <xs | s | m | l | xl>

downstream: [prd:<phase>]     # which PRD version consumes this
covers: [<existing PRD FRs this spec changes or builds on>]
depends_on: [<spec ids that must land first>]
                              # jira_* — required when the profile's `downstream` includes jira; otherwise '-'
jira_project: <the product's jira_project in the profile>   # so the ticket can be filed without re-deriving it
jira_issuetype: <Task | Bug>  # Task by default; Bug only for a true regression

jira_priority: <one of the profile's jira.priorities>

  - '<which box> — <why it was accepted> — <who decided>, <YYYY-MM-DD>'

  - '<date> <source> — <one line on what it contributed>'

```

**Statuses.**

| Status | Means |
|---|---|
| `draft` | Captured, not worked yet |
| `eliciting` | Being worked with the decider |
| `ready` | Section 16 is fully ticked — may go to development |
| `filed` | Handed off: the ticket exists, or the PRD version has taken it |
| `parked` | Deliberately paused, expected to come back |
| `buried` | Dead — should not come back |

`parked` and `buried` both need `status_reason`. On a backlog that runs for years the same idea
returns, and the reason is what stops it being elaborated again from scratch — or rebuilt after it
was killed on purpose. When a parked spec comes back, move it to `eliciting` and keep the old reason
in section 6 as a dated decision.

### 1. Need

**Problem.** Who feels the pain, when, and why it matters. Two to four sentences. Quote the client
verbatim where you can.

**Outcome.** What changes for the user, observable, no implementation.

*If one concept does the heavy lifting — a distinction the whole spec rests on — put it here as a
two-column table. NWE's `Partner ≠ Exhibitor` table is the model: it stops every later rule being
re-argued.*

### 2. Business value

Two or three sentences. Why the business wants it: friction removed, risk reduced, revenue,
compliance. Name the regulation if there is one. No invented metrics.

*This is the section the PRD needs and the one NWE specs do not have. Keep it short; it is not a
business case.*

### 3. Rules

Numbered `R1`, `R2`, … so the FR Coverage Map, the PRD and the stories can all cite the same id.
Ids never change meaning — if a rule is withdrawn, strike it through and keep the number.

Each rule is one testable statement. If it cannot be checked without reading code, rewrite it.

Every rule carries its source in brackets at the end:

- `[src: <intake file>#<line or timestamp>]` — the client asked for it
- `[design]` — it came out of a design the client approved
- `[BA]` — we decided it; also list it in section 8 as an assumption
- `[PROPOSED]` — we drafted it and nobody has agreed yet; must also be in section 9

*Nothing enters Rules without one of those four. A capability we invented reads as approved on every
later pass unless it is labelled.*

Group rules by entity or surface when there are more than about eight.

### 4. Edge cases and failure paths

**Work the list. Write "n/a" against the ones that genuinely do not apply — an empty line is
indistinguishable from not having looked.**

| # | Case | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Nothing exists yet.** First run, no records, no history. What does the user see, and where does the very first one come from? | |
| E2 | **The world already has data this feature does not know about.** On the day it ships, what is already true elsewhere that this will misreport? | |
| E3 | **The write fails.** The dependent system is down or rejects it. Does the save fail, retry, or half-succeed? What does the user see? | |
| E4 | **Required related data is missing.** The parent record, the linked account, the id does not exist yet. | |
| E5 | **Duplicate.** The same thing submitted twice, or already there. | |
| E6 | **Deletion.** What happens to this when its parent is deleted, and what happens to its children when it is? | |
| E7 | **Limits.** Longest allowed text, biggest number, most rows. What happens at the boundary and past it? | |
| E8 | **Two people at once.** Same record, same moment. Last write wins, or blocked? | |
| E9 | **Not permitted.** What does a user without the right see — hidden, disabled with a reason, or an error? Per role. | |
| E10 | **Anything else this feature can reach.** Timezones, currency rounding, partial saves, file types. | |

*E1 and E2 come from walking it cold: throw away the imagined data, open it on day one, and narrate
what the first record's life looks like. This one exercise produced four of these ten classes on the
quick-note trial. E9 is the most-missed of all — 3% of AcmeLending specs and 6% of NWE's address it.*

Anything this section cannot answer goes to section 9.

### 5. Out of scope — and deliberate removals

Two lists, because they are read differently downstream.

**Not in this spec** — what a reader might reasonably assume is included and is not.

**Deliberately removed** — things that exist today, or were previously specced, that this
deliberately drops. State these plainly so the stories can repeat them and nobody reads the absence
as a regression. Say where the decision is recorded.

*Distinguish **retired** from **deferred**. Deferred comes back and keeps its evidence; retired
should not come back, and saying so stops it being re-scoped in six months.*

### 6. Decisions

| Date | Who | Decision | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | | *verbatim quote, or a reference* |

Every decision says who made it. Where the call was ours rather than the client's, say so.

*Keep closed questions here rather than deleting them. The reasoning is the asset.*

### 7. Conflicts

**Checked against:** list what you actually compared this to — shipped behaviour, other specs by id,
settled decisions elsewhere.

Then either "no collision", or each conflict with both sides quoted and how it resolved.

*Record the near-misses too — the things that look like a contradiction and are not, so they are not
re-derived. "Empty is a valid answer; none found is better than silence."*

### 8. Assumptions

**Confirmed** — the client agreed this, on a date, in a named source.

**Assumed** — we decided it and nobody has confirmed. Every line here must also appear in section 9.

*The split is the point. A guess stated in the same voice as an agreed decision gets built.*

### 9. Open

| # | Question | Owner | Criticality | Raised | Closed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | client / BA / dev | critical / non-critical | | |

**Critical** means development cannot start. Owner is who actually has to answer, not who noticed.

**Only questions someone else must answer go here** — the client, or a developer. If the BA can
decide it, decide it while writing and record it in section 6 as `[BA]`. In the trial, most open
questions were answered by the BA in one line each; listing them made the spec look far less settled
than it was, and buried the few that really needed the client.

Before adding a question, check it is not already answered — in the policy or source document, an
earlier call, or a closed item in another spec. Do not turn a line of policy wording into a question
when its meaning is already clear.

An empty list is a meaningful state — say "None" rather than deleting the section.

*Open questions must survive into the PRD's "Open Items Carried Into Build". A question dropped here
is a question the developer discovers.*

### 10. Data model — *when it adds or changes stored data*

Entities with real field names. Mark each new, changed or unchanged. Say whether a migration is
needed, and if not, say "no migration — all net-new".

### 11. Roles and permissions — *when behaviour differs by role*

A matrix, roles across the top, actions down the side. Include a row for **what the denied user
sees**, not just a cross.

**Columns are the product's real roles — all of them, using their on-screen names.** Never invent a
role or split one the product does not split. Name the code role in brackets the first time.

**The role list comes from the project profile** — `products[].roles` in
`docs/BA/specs/.ba-profile.yml`: each role's on-screen name, its code name, the code file it was
checked against, and any notes (for example, whether a word like "Staff" is a real role or only a
group name in that product). **The code is the source of record;** if the code and the profile
disagree, fix the profile first.

**Every cell carries one of four values:**

- ✅ or ❌ **with its source tag** — `[src: …]`, `[design]` or `[BA]`, as in section 3
- `unchanged` — this change does not touch the role
- `?` — nobody has said. **Every `?` also goes into section 9 (Open)**, or is decided as `[BA]`

*Why: developers build this table as permissions and testers test it. In the trial, a matrix was
drawn with invented columns ("Staff", "Reviewer") and without the Agent at all, because the sources
only said "the customer or a staff member". A `?` makes that gap visible instead of filling it with
a plausible guess.*

| Action | *one column per role in the profile, in the profile's order* |
|---|---|

### 12. Non-functional requirements — *when any apply*

Only what is genuinely different from the platform default. Delete the rest.

- **Volume** — how many records, how often, at what growth
- **Speed** — anything a user waits for, and the acceptable wait
- **Audit** — what must be recorded, who did it, retained how long
- **Privacy** — personal data touched, who may see it, what leaves the system
- **Availability** — what happens when a dependency is down (cross-check E3)

*`epics.md` has a NonFunctional Requirements section. Nothing currently feeds it.*

### 13. UX — *when there are screens*

- **Where the designs are** — file, page, and frame ids, per screen
- **Still to draw** — an explicit list; a missing frame is not the same as a decided one
- Anything undecided is written **on the frame itself**, so a design review cannot approve an open
  question by accident

### 14. Existing code to reuse — *always, on a product that already ships*

Checked at `<repo> @ <sha> (<branch>, <date>)` — **one line per repo you looked in.**

**Before you look: fetch, and read the newest development branch — never the local checkout as it
stands.** Local copies go stale without warning: in the trial, the frontend checkout was the June
`prod` build, so every path in the old spec looked right and was not. Run `git fetch`, then read the
branch the developers merge into — `code.dev_branch` in the profile — with
`git show origin/<branch>:<path>` or `git grep … origin/<branch>`. When the question is "what is live
today", read `code.live_branch` too. If a feature branch for this work already exists, check that too.
The repos to look in are `code.repos` in the profile.

- **Already built and reused** — path, and what it does
- **To extend** — path, and what changes
- **Do not touch** — path, and why
- **Nothing found** — say so explicitly

*Half the value here is discovering the work is smaller than it looked. On the quick-note trial the
whole send-to-CRM path already existed in production. This section is what makes a spec a build
order rather than a description, and it feeds the story's Dev Notes directly.*

### 15. Slicing — *when it is larger than `s`*

| # | Slice | Value on its own | Blocked by |
|---|---|---|---|

*A slice with no value on its own is not a slice, it is a task.*

### 16. Ready checklist

`status: ready` means all of these are true. It is the point at which the spec may enter a PRD
version and go to development. Tick them explicitly.

- [ ] Section 9 (Open) is empty, or every remaining question is recorded as knowingly accepted, with who decided
- [ ] Every assumption in section 8 is either confirmed or listed as accepted
- [ ] Section 4 (Edge cases) has an answer or "n/a" against every row
- [ ] The conflict check in section 7 has actually been run, and says what it was run against
- [ ] Every rule in section 3 carries a source tag
- [ ] The design exists for every screen, or the gap is listed under "still to draw"
- [ ] Section 14 was checked against a named commit
- [ ] `size` and `downstream` are set

*If a box cannot be ticked, the spec is `eliciting`, not `ready`. Accepting a blocker you can name
is a decision; letting one through silently is not.*

**Filing before every box is ticked.** Sometimes a spec must be filed anyway — the work is already
scheduled, or the open part does not affect the build. That is allowed, but only in the open: add one
line per unticked box to `accepted_risks` in the header — which box, why, who decided, and the date.
In the trial, both filed specs had unticked boxes and neither said so. The rule keeps the gate strict
and makes each exception visible.

## What this costs

More than any of the three today. Honest estimate for a medium feature: half a day rather than two
hours, most of it in sections 4 and 14.

Sections 4 and 14 are where the extra time goes and they are also where it comes back — edge cases
found here cost one line, and found in QA cost a round trip. Section 14 has repeatedly shown the
work is smaller than it looked.

For a small bug fix, use size `xs`: sections 1, 3, 5, 6 and 16. Do not run the whole thing on a
one-line change.

## How it is used

- **Every project that installs ba-tools** uses this template for new specs. Existing specs stay in
  their old format; convert one only when it is reopened.
- **Project facts live in the profile** (`docs/BA/specs/.ba-profile.yml`), never in this file. The
  first projects: Acme Lending + Bond Portal (`acme-docs`, from 2026-09-23) and NWE (from
  2026-09-24).
- **Client briefs** (`feature-brief`) are written *from* a spec. They are not a second template.
- **Tools that start a spec** — `intake-and-triage` (from a client call) and `fetch-jira-backlog`
  (from an existing ticket) — write this template's header and hand the file to the `ba-spec` skill,
  which writes the rest.
